\subsection{Experiment Setup} 
\label{sec:setup}

\subsubsection{Hardware and Software Platforms} 

We evaluate MAGSAT on two GPU platforms: the NVIDIA A100 (Ampere architecture) and the H100 (Hopper architecture). 
These two platforms represent recent NVIDIA GPU generations with distinct differences in hardware resources, 
enabling us to assess whether the proposed framework remains effective across hardware generations. 
All experiments are conducted on machines running Ubuntu 22.04, with CUDA 12.6 and PyTorch 2.9.0.

\subsubsection{Comparison Configurations and Methods}

We evaluate both operator-level and model-level workloads. At the
operator level, we use representative GEMM and convolution workloads,
which cover the compute-intensive primitives commonly appearing in
Transformer and CNN models. At the model level, we evaluate four
representative neural networks: BERT~\cite{devlin2019bert},
LLaMA~\cite{touvron2023llama}, ResNet~\cite{he2016resnet}, and
AlexNet~\cite{krizhevsky2012alexnet}. These models exercise both
GEMM-dominated and convolution-dominated computation patterns.

We compare MAGSAT with five baselines. \textbf{Torch Native} denotes eager-mode
PyTorch execution. \textbf{torch.compile} denotes the PyTorch
2.x compiler stack based on TorchDynamo and
TorchInductor~\cite{ansel2024pytorch}. \textbf{CUTLASS} is NVIDIA's
template-based GPU kernel library~\cite{nvidia22cutlass}.
\textbf{DietCode}~\cite{zheng2022dietcode} and
\textbf{Helix}~\cite{zhou2025helix} are state-of-the-art systems in
dynamic-shape tensor program compilation. All experiments use FP16
inputs and outputs. For timing, we perform 100 warm-up iterations before
measurement and report the average performance.



\subsection{Operator Performance}
\label{sec:operatorperf}

We first evaluate MAGSAT at the operator level using \texttt{GEMM}- and
\texttt{Conv2D}-based workloads on both A100 and H100 GPUs. These
workloads cover the dominant computation patterns in Transformer and CNN
inference, including plain operators and common fused variants such as
\texttt{GEMM}-\texttt{bias}-\texttt{activation},
\texttt{GEMM}-\texttt{bias}-\texttt{layernorm},
\texttt{Conv}-\texttt{ReLU}, and
\texttt{Conv}-\texttt{BN}-\texttt{Add}-\texttt{ReLU}. 
Figure~\ref{fig:op_level_performance} presents the operator-level
performance across these fused workloads on both A100 and H100.
On A100, MAGSAT achieves an average speedup of 2.84$\times$ over Torch Native for
\texttt{GEMM}, with a maximum speedup of 3.93$\times$. For convolution
fusion, MAGSAT improves \texttt{Conv}-\texttt{ReLU} by 2.36$\times$ on
average over Torch Native and 2.82$\times$ over \texttt{torch}.\texttt{compile};
for \texttt{Conv}-\texttt{BN}-\texttt{Add}-\texttt{ReLU}, the average
speedups further increase to 4.55$\times$ and 4.73$\times$, respectively. 
On H100, the fused GEMM operators show stronger performance. For
\texttt{GEMM}-\texttt{bias}-\texttt{activation}, MAGSAT achieves an
average speedup of 4.02$\times$ over Torch Native, with a maximum speedup of
5.06$\times$. For \texttt{GEMM}-\texttt{bias}-\texttt{layernorm}, it
achieves an average speedup of 2.17$\times$ and a maximum speedup of
2.58$\times$. For convolution fusion on H100, MAGSAT improves
\texttt{Conv}-\texttt{ReLU} by 1.98$\times$ on average over Torch Native 
and 3.22$\times$ over \texttt{torch}.\texttt{compile}, while 
\texttt{Conv}-\texttt{BN}-\texttt{Add}-\texttt{ReLU} achieves average
speedups of 5.33$\times$ and 2.97$\times$, respectively. These gains are
most visible for small and irregular shapes, where generic library kernels
and compiler-generated kernels often suffer from launch overhead,
conservative schedule choices, or insufficient specialization for the
current tensor shape.

Overall, the operator-level results show that MAGSAT improves not only
individual kernel execution but also fused operator execution under
dynamic shapes. The gains on fused GEMM and convolution variants indicate
that MAGSAT's tuning strategy can select schedules that exploit both the
current problem size and the surrounding post-operator computation,
reducing intermediate memory traffic and avoiding unnecessary kernel
launches.

\subsection{End-to-end Performance}
\label{sec:end2end}

We next evaluate whether the operator-level improvements translate into
end-to-end model acceleration. For Transformer workloads, we evaluate
BERT-base and LLaMA-base under dynamic sequence lengths. On A100, MAGSAT
achieves an average latency speedup of 2.27$\times$ over Torch Native
and 1.71$\times$ over \texttt{torch}.\texttt{compile} on BERT-base. On
LLaMA-base, the average speedups increase to 5.21$\times$ over Torch
Native and 1.90$\times$ over \texttt{torch}.\texttt{compile}. The same
trend holds on H100. MAGSAT achieves 3.47$\times$ and 2.91$\times$
average speedup over Torch Native and \texttt{torch}.\texttt{compile} on
BERT-base, respectively, and 4.10$\times$ and 2.50$\times$ on LLaMA-base.
Figure~\ref{fig:e2e_performance} summarizes the end-to-end performance
across Transformer and CNN workloads on both A100 and H100.
These results show that MAGSAT is effective for GEMM-dominated models
with dynamic sequence lengths, where each input length induces different
matrix shapes and therefore benefits from shape-adaptive schedule
selection.

For CNN workloads, we evaluate AlexNet and ResNet-101 under dynamic batch
sizes. On A100, MAGSAT achieves an average throughput speedup of
1.62$\times$ over Torch Native and 1.23$\times$ over
\texttt{torch}.\texttt{compile} on AlexNet, and 2.31$\times$ and
1.30$\times$ on ResNet-101. On H100, MAGSAT further achieves
1.76$\times$ and 1.38$\times$ average speedup on AlexNet, and
2.46$\times$ and 1.35$\times$ on ResNet-101. The speedups on CNNs are
more moderate than those on LLMs because convolution backends are already
highly optimized for many regular shapes. Nevertheless, MAGSAT still
consistently outperforms both Torch Native and \texttt{torch}.\texttt{compile},
especially on the deeper ResNet-101 model, where the benefits of
operator fusion and per-shape tuning accumulate across many convolution
layers.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}

We use ablation experiments to quantify each MAGSAT component. The
experiments cover both LLM and CNN workloads.
We compare three variants. \textbf{Full} enables operator fusion and
shape-adaptive configuration selection. \textbf{Fusion only} keeps
fusion but uses one fixed tile configuration for all shapes.
\textbf{Tuning only} disables fusion while retaining the analytical cost
model for per-shape tuning. All variants are evaluated against
\textbf{Torch Native}. For LLMs, we vary sequence length from 1 to 253
with batch size 1. For CNNs, we vary batch size from 1 to 64.

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} shows the speedup of each configuration
over Torch Native. Speedup is normalized to Torch Native (dashed line at 1.0).
On BERT-base and LLaMA-base, MAGSAT (full) achieves
3.3--4.7$\times$ and 3.6--5.1$\times$ speedup, respectively.
Removing adaptive tuning (fusion only) reduces performance by
0.3--1.0$\times$, especially at larger sequence lengths,
confirming that shape-adaptive configuration selection is essential
for dynamic workloads.
Removing fusion (tuning only) further degrades performance to
2.4--3.1$\times$ on BERT and 2.5--3.8$\times$ on LLaMA,
demonstrating that operator fusion contributes significantly
to end-to-end latency reduction by eliminating intermediate
memory traffic.
On CNNs, MAGSAT (full) achieves 1.7--1.9$\times$ on AlexNet
and 2.4--2.7$\times$ on ResNet-101.
Notably, tuning only occasionally underperforms Torch Native
on AlexNet (e.g., batch sizes 20 and 32),
indicating that without fusion, the kenrel with static config shows 
inferior performance compared to Torch Native implementation under certain shapes.
These results validate that both fusion and shape-adaptive tuning
are necessary to achieve robust performance across diverse models
and dynamic shapes.

\subsection{Parameter Tuning Efficiency}
\label{sec:tuning}

% 选到的配置相比全局最优配置的性能差别，以及在各种形状下取到最优配置的比例
To evaluate the efficiency of our tuning mechanism, we measure the performance gap between the configurations selected by MAGSAT and the per-shape global optimums obtained through exhaustive search. We focus on \texttt{GEMM} and its fused variants as representative case studies, given their dominance as the most critical and time-consuming components in modern Transformer-based dynamic shape workloads. Across a diverse set of 85 dynamic shapes, our tuner identifies configurations for the standard \texttt{GEMM} operator with a mean performance gap of 18.32\% relative to the theoretical oracle. More notably, when applied to complex fused kernels, this gap narrows significantly to 11.72\% for \texttt{GEMM\_bias\_layernorm} and 12.08\% for \texttt{GEMM\_bias\_act}. This reduction occurs due to the shifted performance bottleneck and the constrained valid search space in fused epilogues. Specifically, fusing operations like \texttt{layernorm} introduces thread-block reductions that inherently increase register pressure and shared memory consumption. This architectural constraint heavily prunes the valid search space by eliminating overly large tile sizes that would otherwise cause register spilling, effectively flattening the performance landscape and allowing the tuner to converge near the global optimum more easily. Ultimately, by accepting a marginal 11\% to 18\% performance trade-off compared to the oracle, our tuner successfully bypasses the prohibitively expensive exhaustive search process, delivering a highly practical and efficient compilation strategy for dynamic shapes.
